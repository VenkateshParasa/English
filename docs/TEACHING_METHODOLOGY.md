# 🧑‍🏫 Teaching Methodology

Why the app behaves the way it does — and the rules any new exercise must follow.
This is the pedagogy contract. [CURRICULUM.md](CURRICULUM.md) says *what* we teach;
this says *how*.

---

## 1. Principles

**1. Production over recognition.**
Picking the right answer from four options is easier than producing language and does
not transfer to speech. Multiple choice is a warm-up or a check, never the main event.
Every session must end with the learner having *said something* that was not read off
the screen.

**2. Feedback must teach, not just judge.**
"Wrong" is worthless. A learner who chooses *is* in "I ___ happy today" needs:
> ✗ *is* is for he / she / it. With **I**, use **am** — and in speech it's almost always
> the contraction: *I'm happy today.*

Rule: every wrong answer returns a *reason*, a *contrast*, and a *retry*.

**3. Never claim more accuracy than we have.**
Browser speech recognition tells us what the recogniser guessed, not whether a human
would understand the learner. So we report what actually happened
("the recogniser heard *ship*") and never say "Perfect!" for a substring match.
Overstated praise is the fastest way to fossilise an error.

**4. Errors are the syllabus.**
What a learner gets wrong is more informative than what they get right. Mistakes
should be logged by *type* (article omission, /v/–/w/ confusion, past-tense agreement)
and resurfaced through spaced repetition. A learner should be able to see
"your top 5 recurring mistakes this month".

**5. Little and often beats long and rare.**
Short daily sessions with spaced review outperform weekly marathons. The streak counter
already reflects this; the review queue is what makes it work.

**6. Meaningful input only.**
A grammatically valid sentence is not automatically useful English. *The penguin
meanders madly at the estuary* is generated by the current templates and is valid,
memorable, and useless. Practice sentences must be things a learner might plausibly
say. Combinatorial generators buy volume at the cost of usefulness — use them to pad,
never as the primary content.

**7. Notice, then practise, then use.**
Every teaching item follows this arc:
1. **Notice** — see or hear the pattern in context, with the target highlighted.
2. **Practise** — controlled drill where only the target varies.
3. **Use** — an open task where the learner chooses whether to use it.
Skipping step 3 produces learners who pass tests and cannot talk.

## 2. Feedback rules by exercise type

### Vocabulary check
- Right: confirm, then extend — show one collocation and one word-family member.
- Wrong: show the correct definition *next to* the chosen one and name the difference.
- Always schedule the word in SRS (`SRS.schedule(word, correct)`) — this already happens
  at `app.js:1443`.

### Grammar item
- Right on first try: schedule the grammar point at a longer interval, say nothing more.
- Wrong: state the rule in one sentence, give a minimal contrast pair, offer a retry,
  and reset that point's SRS interval to 1 day.
- Never mark a defensible alternative wrong. If both *I've eaten* and *I ate* work in the
  context, the item is badly written — fix the item, not the learner.

### Pronunciation — discrimination (hear it, pick it)
- This is the only pronunciation task we can grade reliably offline. Prefer it.
- Wrong: replay both words back to back, slowed, and say which feature differs
  ("/iː/ is longer and the lips are wider than /ɪ/").
- Track accuracy per phoneme pair, not per item — that is the learner's real profile.

### Pronunciation — production (record yourself)
- Never score it automatically. Give a **rubric** and the model audio:
  - Did I stress the right syllable?
  - Did I reduce the unstressed vowels, or pronounce every letter?
  - Did I link the words, or say them one by one?
  - Did my pitch fall at the end of the statement?
- Keep the recording. Progress over weeks is the reward here, not a score today.

### Read-aloud with speech recognition
- Compare transcript to target **word by word** and highlight mismatches.
- Report honestly: "The recogniser missed 2 of 9 words: *asked*, *texts*." Those are
  usually consonant clusters — link straight to the relevant drill.
- Never print "Perfect!" unless every word matched, and even then phrase it as
  "The recogniser understood every word" — which is what we actually know.

### Listening comprehension
- Ask a **gist** question before any detail question; play once for gist, twice for detail.
- On a wrong answer, replay only the relevant clip, then show the transcript. Transcript
  last, always — revealing it early converts listening practice into reading practice.

### Free speaking
- No grading. Prompt, timer, recording, self-review rubric, and a fluency number
  (words per minute, filler count).
- Compare against the learner's own past performance, never against a native baseline.

## 3. Spaced repetition policy

The scheduler is a simplified SM-2 in `js/core/srs.js`, keyed by lowercase word,
persisted to `localStorage` under `srsData`.

Current scope: **vocabulary only**. It should also carry:

| Item type | Key | Reset trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary word | `vocab:happy` | wrong definition, or read-aloud failure |
| Grammar point | `gram:present-perfect` | wrong item on that point |
| Phoneme pair | `phon:iː-ɪ` | wrong discrimination choice |
| Collocation | `coll:make-a-decision` | wrong or unused in production task |

Intervals: a lapse resets to 1 day and drops ease; success moves 1 → 3 → 7 → 16 → 35
days. Do not let the queue exceed ~20 items a day — an unmanageable backlog is the most
common reason learners abandon SRS apps. Cap the daily queue and defer the rest.

---

## 4. Level progression

Promote a learner when, over the last 10 sessions at their current level, they hold:
- ≥ 85% first-try accuracy on grammar items,
- ≥ 80% on pronunciation discrimination,
- and have completed ≥ 5 free-speaking tasks.

Demotion is never automatic. Offer easier content, never announce a downgrade.

---

## 5. Tone

Some rules about voice, because the app talks to a nervous adult learner:

- Address the learner as *you*; never use baby talk or excessive exclamation marks.
- Praise the specific thing: "your word stress on *photographer* was right" beats "Great job!"
- Frame errors as normal and temporary. "Most learners take a while with articles" is
  true and it keeps people practising.
- Never use a red ✗ without the fix on the same screen.
- Do not gamify at the expense of honesty. A streak that survives a session where the
  learner got everything wrong teaches nothing.

---

## 6. Accessibility is pedagogy

A learner who cannot hear the model cannot learn the sound; a learner who cannot read
the small IPA cannot use it. Practically:

- Every audio item needs a transcript available on demand (after the listening attempt).
- IPA must be selectable and legible at 200% zoom, with a plain-English gloss available
  ("/ʃ/ — the *sh* in *ship*") for learners who never learned the symbols.
- Every drill must be completable by keyboard alone; speaking tasks need a
  skip-and-mark-done path for learners who cannot record.
